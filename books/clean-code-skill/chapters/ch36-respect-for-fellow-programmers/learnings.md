# Chapter 36: Respect for Fellow Programmers

This chapter presents the ninth promise of the Programmer's Oath: judge fellow programmers only by their professional merit — their ethics, standards, disciplines, and skill — and by nothing else. The profession is a community of mutual respect whose sole entry qualification is craft.

## The Ninth Promise

"I will respect my fellow programmers for their ethics, standards, disciplines, and skill. No other attribute or characteristic will be a factor in my regard for my fellow programmers." This is an oath, not a suggestion: respect among professionals is earned through craft alone, and granted on no other basis.

## Only Professional Merit Counts

Evaluate colleagues exclusively on the four professional attributes: ethics, standards, disciplines, and skill. Software professionals accept the "weighty burden" of the craft, and the only qualifications for entry into the community — and for receiving each member's acceptance and respect — are the skills, disciplines, standards, and ethics of the profession; no personal trait counts.

## No Discrimination on Any Other Basis

Programmers come in every human variety — every race, sex, orientation, political affiliation, and belief system. None of these attributes is "worthy of consideration" when regarding a fellow programmer, and "no discrimination on any other basis can be tolerated." The community's acceptance must be open to anyone who meets its professional qualifications.

## "Programmers first"

"Within our profession, we are programmers first." Whatever identities or affiliations people hold outside the work, inside the profession the shared identity of craftsperson takes precedence. The chapter closes on this line, letting it stand alone.

## In practice

- When reviewing code, critiquing a design, or weighing someone's technical opinion, consider only the work's ethics, standards, discipline, and skill — never the author's identity, background, politics, or beliefs.
- Extend acceptance and respect to anyone who demonstrates professional craft; withhold judgment on every other human attribute.
- Do not tolerate discrimination in the team or community on any non-professional basis.
- Critique work, not people: hold colleagues to professional standards while treating them as full members of the community.
- In professional settings, lead with the shared identity of programmer rather than personal differences.
