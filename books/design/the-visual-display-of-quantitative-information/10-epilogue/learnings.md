# Epilogue: Designs for the Display of Information

The Epilogue is a one-page closing statement (p. 191). Its central argument: design is choice, the book's principles exist to generate and guide those choices rather than to dictate them, and the ultimate goal of all information design is the clear portrayal of complexity.

## Design is choice

- "Design is choice." The theory of the visual display of quantitative information consists of principles that do two things: they **generate design options** and they **guide choices among options** (p. 191).
- Treat principles as tools, not laws. They "should not be applied rigidly or in a peevish spirit; they are not logically or mathematically certain" (p. 191).
- Elegance outranks rule-following: "it is better to violate any principle than to place graceless or inelegant marks on paper" (p. 191). When a rule from this book (maximize data-ink, erase non-data-ink, avoid chartjunk, shape graphics wider than tall, etc.) collides with grace and elegance in a particular design, let the rule give way.
- Stay skeptical of stated rules, including Tufte's own: "Most principles of design should be greeted with some skepticism, for word authority can dominate our vision, and we may come to see only through the lenses of word authority rather than with our own eyes" (p. 191). Judge a design by looking at it, not by checking it against a verbal checklist.

## The goal: revelation of the complex

- "What is to be sought in designs for the display of information is the clear portrayal of complexity. Not the complication of the simple; rather the task of the designer is to give visual access to the subtle and the difficult — that is, the revelation of the complex." (p. 191)
- Actionable reading of this closing principle:
  - Do not dress up simple data to look complicated (no puffed-up displays, no decoration masquerading as content).
  - Do take on genuinely complex, subtle, difficult data, and design so the viewer can actually see its structure.
  - Measure success by whether the design reveals complexity clearly, not by how simple or how ornate it looks.

## How this frames the whole book

The Epilogue recasts everything preceding it — graphical excellence and integrity (Part I) and the theory of data graphics (data-ink maximization, erasing, chartjunk avoidance, multifunctioning elements, data density, aesthetics; Part II) — as a vocabulary of options and a set of defeasible guides for choice, all in service of one end: giving viewers visual access to the complex (p. 191).
