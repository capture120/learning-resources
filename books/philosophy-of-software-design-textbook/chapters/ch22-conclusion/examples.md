# Chapter 22: Conclusion — Examples

This chapter is a two-page summary and contains **no code examples, case studies, or war stories**. It only references, by name, concepts and red flags worked out in earlier chapters. Those references are listed below so a reader can route to the chapters where the actual examples live.

## Concepts cited as the book's recap (no new detail given here)

- **Root causes of complexity: dependencies and obscurity** — developed in Chapter 2.
- **Red flags for detecting unnecessary complexity** — the chapter names three: *information leakage* (Chapter 5), *unneeded error conditions* (Chapter 10), and *names that are too generic* (Chapter 14).
- **Techniques for simpler systems** — the chapter names three: *deep and generic classes* (Chapters 4 and 6), *defining errors out of existence* (Chapter 10), and *separating interface documentation from implementation documentation* (Chapter 13).
- **The investment mindset** — developed in Chapter 3 (tactical vs. strategic programming).

Each reference demonstrates the chapter's single point: all of the book's specific advice is in service of the one goal of reducing complexity, and applying it is an investment that pays off quickly, becomes cheap with practice, and makes development more enjoyable.
