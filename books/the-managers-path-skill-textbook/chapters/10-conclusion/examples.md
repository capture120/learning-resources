# Conclusion — Examples and Anecdotes

## The meditations cut from the first draft

**Situation:** Fournier wanted to convey how central self-awareness is to every stage of the management path.

**What happened / what was done:** Her first draft of the book included a series of meditations at each career level. Though they were removed, she kept the underlying recommendation: meditation practice was essential to developing her own self-management and self-awareness, and she suggests readers try it, pointing to the podcasts at tarabrach.com and the writings of Pema Chödrön as starting points.

**Lesson:** Recognizing the voice of your own ego is a trainable skill, and meditation is one practical way to build it — useful but not a cure-all.

## The morning journaling habit and the "Get curious" mantra

**Situation:** Fournier needed a daily practice to clear her mind, manage her ego, and prepare for the day as a leader.

**What happened / what was done:** Every morning she writes a page or two of free-flow thoughts, and she always ends the entry with the mantra "Get curious."

**Lesson:** A small, consistent self-reflection ritual keeps ego in check and primes a leader to approach the day with curiosity rather than fixed interpretations.

## Her coach's standing advice in frustrating situations

**Situation:** Fournier describes becoming a great leader as a series of difficult lessons, mistakes, and challenges. She was often frustrated by the interpersonal situations she found herself in.

**What happened / what was done:** Whenever she brought one of these situations to her coach, the coach gave the same advice every time: think about it from the other person's perspective. What are they trying to do? What do they value? What do they want and need? In short — get curious.

**Lesson:** Frustration with people is usually a signal that you are stuck inside your own story. Deliberately reconstructing the other person's goals, values, and needs is the reliable way out, and the same curiosity should be applied to process, technology, strategy, and business.
