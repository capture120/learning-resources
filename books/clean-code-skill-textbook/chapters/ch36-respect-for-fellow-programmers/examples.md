# Chapter 36: Respect for Fellow Programmers — Examples

This chapter contains no code examples, case studies, or war stories. It is a one-page statement of the ninth promise of the Programmer's Oath, argued as a creed rather than illustrated with scenarios. The argumentative thread is summarized below.

### The argumentative thread: a brotherhood defined by craft alone

The chapter opens with the oath itself: "I will respect my fellow programmers for their ethics, standards, disciplines, and skill. No other attribute or characteristic will be a factor in my regard for my fellow programmers."

The author then makes the case in three moves:

1. **Shared burden.** Software professionals accept the "weighty burden" of the craft. That burden is carried by humans of every kind — the author deliberately enumerates the diversity: men and women, straight and gay, "black, brown, yellow, and white," republicans and democrats, religious believers and atheists.
2. **Single qualification.** The profession is "a community — a brotherhood — of mutual respect," and the only qualifications for entry and for receiving each member's acceptance and respect are the skills, disciplines, standards, and ethics of the profession. No other human attribute is worthy of consideration, and discrimination on any other basis cannot be tolerated.
3. **Identity precedence.** The closing line anchors the lesson: "Within our profession, we are programmers first." Inside the work, the shared identity of craftsperson overrides every personal difference.

The lesson it anchors: respect is earned through professional craft and granted on no other basis — judge the work and the discipline behind it, never the person's identity.
