i think the reality is that people are people (humans) and are emotional, even when they don't think they are. and like emotion is good, I wouldn't give that up, but young people tend to have a lot of pride and ambition, older people are set in their ways, etc.

it is a good skill to take feedback well, but it's an even greater skill to give feedback well. my favorite book on this topic is called "thanks for feedback".

crucially, it's even harder to improve without feedback, just by interacting with the environment and learning from unspoken feedback (from mistakes, from people passing on you etc). like imagine supervised learning vs unsupervised

the unspoken feedback unfortunately requires high EQ and perception of social situations.
i think eq is trainable to a degree
