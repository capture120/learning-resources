# Truth Seeking

be a truth seeker.

question myself constantly.

hold deep convictions intuited from first principles to abstract thinking.

be able to explain every step leading to my conviction.

if any of these steps are challenged in a meaningful way, i should understand the challenge and see if it matches my foundational knowledge.

questioning yourself and doubting your foundations doesn't mean you *can't* have intuitions.

you acknowledge your intuitions as potentially lossy, and upduate your model with new data.

doing this rigorously develops a foundation of knowledge, core intuitions, and abstractions you can be confident in.

having confidence in this foundation, testing it at regular intervals, being open to new data.. allows you to build on top of this foundation

rather than building it over and over again.


one must acknowledge you can never know everything.

thatd require a simulation of the world.

everything is an approximation in our minds. 

by first acknowleding this, you can truly let information flow into your head.


it's important to recognize when you are tunnel visioned.

it's important to break out of information cycles.


seeking truth is understanding how things are.

seeking truth eliminates ego. because you must truth seek. 

truth seeking is applicable to any human for any field. the term is common in company building and research


the origin of truth seeking comes from human scientists. 

humans have long been scientists. 


# High Agency

high agency: taking consistent, iterative steps to induce change.

after truth seeking, you act on your constructed world model- to satisfy motivations.

high agency is impacting the world by leveraging intuitions youve gained from truth seeking.

having high agency can improve your ability to truth seek - which in turn can enhance your truth seeking.

taking action can be uncomfortable. 

its important to learn how to have high agency.

qualities of high agency:
- sees agency as a skill
- detaches capability from self-worth
- compares against ones past self. not others
- acting despite resistance
- aggressively lowers activation energy
- understanding goals at many layers
- prioritizing to achieve those goals
- knowing failures produce useful insights
- knowing failure is highly common (hard to internalize)
- sees any failure as an opportunity to improve some skill
- rapidly iterating
- gaining data and signal from iterations (small steps as experiments to make course corrections)
- gathering data to inform action - as opposed to acting on first impulse
- breaks down work into subwork
- searches and automates repetitive proccesses


# Environment - Data Ingestion

we, fundamentally as humans, take in data and take actions.

environment produces passive data we take in. it has great influence on our minds.

knowing your environment is important seems intuitive, but many still underestimate its impact.

environment can entail many things. some examples:
- openness of space
- noise levels
- social circle
- presence of other humans
- purpose of space occupied (library, conference room, cubicle)
- presence of nature
- air quality
- objects used for ideation (whiteboards, sticky notes, notebooks, electronic devices)
- temperature
- social media platforms
- having a routine 
- having a calendar
- comfortable or uncomfortable clothing
- awareness of wearing clothes
- the goals you set and visbility of those goals
- any way to represent information in your physical environment
- ease of access to knowledge
