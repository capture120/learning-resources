## notes

- journal once a month
- having a brag document makes you a better engineer
- having a brag document is good because it focuses your mind on impact
- keep to present in promos
- demonstrate repeatable actions that will lead to success later

## good areas:

- biggest user impact
- what have you built / worked on
- had measurable impact: engagement time + revenue + cost + performance
- projects that took long time - complicated difficult twists and turns along the way

## 3 axis when selecting work

- personal involvement
- business impact
- scope (size of project)

## CARL: Context - Action - Result - Learnings

- reflect back on project, what they learned, what will bring on next project, learning from it

- reflections looking for: how deep of insights can you provide. junior engineer gives insights
  “should’ve talked to designer before feature so i could avoid building bad design”

- important that you shared you learned from it : “next time i learned to talk to designer in advance”

- **for senior engineers:** reflect on how you got in situation in first place

- “performance / reliable problem did XYZ do solve it. we wouldn’t have been in this problem
  if we changed this culture or adjusted this metric”

- **forward looking learning.** shipped this feature → noticed XYZ and went to make this change
  → forward learning category

- **revealing weakness + revealing you learned from it = high signal**

- signal of ability to reflect and grow. which weakness you are sharing is important

## Red Flags

### not providing enough clear repeatable actions you did

- junior says built it, shipped it, etc.
- skipping repeatable actions is low signal

### not providing not level of detail.

spending too much context before talking about actions. project fits in some type of archetype
“business feature required by customer” “conflicts with coworker/manager/architect”
“performance reliability challenge”

- too much context can be distracting and delays signal

- need to give enough context s.t hiring manager gets enough signal that action is important

- good interviewers good at pattern matching and just want to get to the signal. dont need to
  simplify so dramatically s.t it is spoon feeding. interviewer will have seen 2-3x more in SLDC.
  good interviewers will stop you and ask for context.

- some ppl would recommend u frontload. in project i did 3 things. let me tell u about context
  and setup for it. can rely on interviewer to ask follow up questions. can rely on interviewer
  to dig on outline if wanted.

### making good first impression.

3 questions common.

- tell me about yourself
- tell me about your most impactful project
- set the stage. theyll remember what you say in the beginning. first 5-10 minutes latching on to
- describe who you are and describe project that matches level you want
- after that is downhill. checking other axis and see if you are right for the role. likely
  have made decision in first 5-10 minutes. especially for staff level. first question will
  determine if person knows where their career. strong candidates know where they are going.
  weaker dont and it is hard to see how theyd be effective

## Framework for Archetypes

3 part frame work to look for.

### 1. brief description bucket in persons mind with personal twist at end

good signal response:

> “hi my name is Austin im a senior engineer with 5 YOE. i spend most of my time on the backend
> and im very passionate about performance”

gives general sense of who this person is and personal aspect want to look into and ask about later

### 2. list of accomplishments:

does candidate understand what theyve accomplished and how theyve gone about adding value.

1. list of things that youve done that has brought about business value. hiring to solve
   business problem in org want real world impact.
2. see have accomplished real world impact.
3. “previous role took on end to end loading problem where we reduced loading problem by 50%
   helped user experience better project.” like bullets off resume in a sense.
4. do not want a history lesson.

### 3. ppl will say 2 above but now want to pass ball back to the interview.

   interviewer after tell me about yourself

## Negative Signal: if they read off a script

a lot of people prepare for behavioral as if coding. they look for list of questions and
practice responses. limited value

**more improtant to understand what value you ahve added to orgs in the past**

what projects youve accomplished. aligning those proejcts to common signal areas (especially
if published values eg zon 16 leadership principles) some collection of stories have spent
little time preparing CARL so you have some idea on how to prepare. first step

**shouldnt prepare for specific questions. prepare for 3.**

1. **tell me about yourself**

2. **favorite project** (expanding on carl format 5-7 minutes if good speaker and organized
   in communication)

3. **conflict story.** time when you resolved a conflict. having well-prepared thought out response.

get a list of stories and see if on video can respond practice using CARL format.

feedback on “if youre clear” “engage by asking simple follow up questions”

> → assemble stories → flesh them out → prepare categories useful for broad variety of questions
> → prepare responses for some common questions → try to answer simulated questions
